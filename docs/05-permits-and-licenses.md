# Permits, Licenses and Registrations

> **Research date:** November 22, 2026. Puerto Rico agencies change portals and fees often. Confirm each step on the agency site and with a Puerto Rico attorney or CPA.

## TL;DR for agents

An AI consulting firm is an office-based professional-services business. It needs **no industry-specific license**. It does need the standard registration stack: **Department of State** (entity) → **IRS EIN** → **Hacienda/SURI** (merchant registry and tax accounts) → **municipality** (*patente*/license) → **CRIM** (personal property) → **Labor/CFSE** (when hiring) → **DDEC** (Act 60 decree). Add **ASG RUL** and **PRITS RPT** if the firm will sell to the Puerto Rico government.

## Step-by-step checklist

| # | Registration | Agency | Cost | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Entity formation** (corporation or LLC) | Departamento de Estado, Registry of Corporations | **$150** | Day 0 | A Puerto Rico LLC is **taxed as a corporation by default**. Keep that for Act 60 (see [09](09-act-60-and-tax-benefits.md)). A foreign (e.g. Delaware) entity needs a **certificate of authorization** to do business in Puerto Rico ($150). A professional corporation form also exists ($150). |
| 2 | **Annual entity maintenance** | Departamento de Estado | LLC: **$150 annual fee by April 15**; corporations file an annual report | Yearly | LLCs file no annual report, only the fee |
| 3 | **Federal EIN** | IRS | Free | After formation | Needed for payroll (FICA/FUTA) and banking |
| 4 | **SURI account + Merchant Registration Certificate** (Certificado de Registro de Comerciante) | Hacienda, via **SURI** (suri.hacienda.pr.gov) | Free | Before starting operations | Every business registers in SURI. The certificate must be displayed. It also sets up IVU, withholding and income-tax accounts |
| 5 | **IVU (sales and use tax) filing** | Hacienda / SURI | n/a | Monthly returns if registered as an IVU collector | See the IVU section below |
| 6 | **Employer withholding registration** (Puerto Rico income tax withholding) | Hacienda / SURI | Free | Before the first payroll | |
| 7 | **Municipal license tax** (*Patente Municipal*) + municipal registration | The municipality where the office is (e.g. San Juan, Guaynabo) | 0.20%–0.50% of gross volume; first-year rules vary | Register within the first months of operations; annual declaration | 50% exempt under a Chapter 3 decree; **100% exempt for 5 years** as a new SME. Some municipalities also require a *Permiso de Uso* for the premises (coworking spaces usually have one) |
| 8 | **Use permit** (*Permiso Único / Permiso de Uso*) | OGPe (Oficina de Gerencia de Permisos) via the Single Business Portal | Varies | Only if leasing a dedicated space | Generally not needed in coworking or for a home office |
| 9 | **Personal property tax return** | **CRIM** (Centro de Recaudación de Ingresos Municipales) | 5.80%–9.83% of assessed value, by municipality | Yearly (May 15 is typical; confirm) | 75% exempt under a decree; 100% for 5 years as a new SME |
| 10 | **Employer registrations** | Puerto Rico Department of Labor (DTRH): unemployment (SUTA), SINOT (disability), Chauffeurs' insurance | See [06-staffing.md](06-staffing.md) | Before the first hire | |
| 11 | **Workers' compensation policy** | **CFSE** (State Insurance Fund), the sole mandatory provider | Premium by payroll class | Before the first hire | Payroll statement due **July 20** each year. A lapse makes the employer directly liable |
| 12 | **Act 60 Chapter 3 decree** | **DDEC**, Incentives Portal | Legal fees + DDEC fee (confirm); **$500 + $5** annual report | Apply **before** serving clients from Puerto Rico | Processing ~60–120 days. Details in [09](09-act-60-and-tax-benefits.md) |
| 13 | **Act 60 Chapter 2 decree** (per founder, optional) | DDEC | **$5,000** application fee; $5,000 annual report + $10,000 donation | File by **Dec 31, 2026** for 0% | |
| 14 | **Government vendor registry: RUL** (Registro Único de Licitadores) | **ASG** (Administración de Servicios Generales), registros.asg.pr.gov | Fee varies | Before bidding on government contracts | Requires a notarized affidavit (Form ASG-633), good-standing certificates (Hacienda, CRIM, Labor, ASUME) and more |
| 15 | **Technology vendor registry: RPT** (Registro de Proveedores de Tecnología) | **PRITS**, administered through ASG | — | Before selling technology services to government | The official database used to qualify any company supplying hardware, software or technology services to the Government of Puerto Rico |
| 16 | **Federal: SAM.gov registration** (optional) | GSA | Free | To sell to federal agencies, or to Puerto Rico agencies spending federal funds | Puerto Rico firms may qualify for **HUBZone** in many areas; check eligibility |

### Good-standing certificates to keep current

Banks, government clients, DDEC and lenders routinely ask for these. Keep them renewed:
- **Hacienda**: tax filing and debt certificates (via SURI)
- **CRIM**: property tax debt certificate
- **Department of State**: certificate of good standing
- **Department of Labor**: unemployment and disability compliance
- **ASUME**: child-support compliance (for government vendors)
- **DDEC**: decree compliance certificate

## IVU (sales and use tax) for a consulting firm

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Standard rate | **11.5%** (10.5% state + 1% municipal) |
| B2B services and designated professional services | **4%** special IVU, paid to Hacienda only (no municipal portion) |
| Small-merchant exclusion | Services by merchants with annual volume **under $50,000** are excluded |
| B2B exclusion | Services to other merchants by providers with annual volume **under $300,000** are excluded (since July 1, 2020) |
| Other exclusions | Advertising and promotion services, intangible rights, and certain services between related decree holders |
| **Open question** | IVU applies to services rendered in Puerto Rico. Confirm with a CPA how **export services billed to non-Puerto Rico clients** are treated (generally expected to fall outside IVU). Also confirm whether consulting to Puerto Rico consumers or businesses falls under the 4% or the 11.5% rate. |

## Sector-specific compliance triggered by clients (not licenses, but required)

- **HIPAA BAAs** for healthcare and health-plan clients.
- **GLBA / FFIEC expectations, OCIF requirements and SR 11-7-style model risk management** for Puerto Rico banks and cooperatives.
- **FDA 21 CFR Part 11 and GxP validation** for life-sciences AI work.
- **The Puerto Rico Government AI Act (SB 68, proposed)** may create a **public registry of businesses using AI** and require reviews for AI used by government. Monitor its status.
- **SOC 2** is not legally required but increasingly expected by mainland clients. Budget for it in year 2.
