## Executive summary

1. **The tax case is strong but narrow.** An Act 60 **Chapter 3** decree taxes export-services profit at **4%**, or **2% for the first 5 years** for a new SME. Distributions to owners are **0%**, and property and municipal taxes are 75%/50% exempt (100% for 5 years as a new SME). Without a decree, corporate rates run up to **37.5%**. The decree only covers **clients outside Puerto Rico, for work with no Puerto Rico nexus**.
2. **The federal benefit depends on structure and residency.** A bona fide resident owner, a Puerto Rico entity **taxed as a corporation**, and work **performed on the island** generally means no federal income tax on that income (IRC §933). A pass-through election costs about **$31.6k/yr per $300k** of profit in SE tax, and on-site mainland work is U.S.-source income.
3. **The market runs at two speeds.** Puerto Rico enterprises are adopting GenAI (IDC: **65%**; **97%** increasing spend; the barrier is data readiness), while only **15.7%** of businesses overall use AI (Census, Oct 2026, vs 24.6% in the U.S.). Pharma and life sciences (about 30% of GDP) and reshoring are big vertical opportunities.
4. **Recommended model: an export-led core plus a local beachhead.** Sell to mainland mid-market, life-sciences global functions and U.S. Hispanic/LatAm companies (tax-advantaged), while building local enterprise, SME and government references (regular tax rates). Keep separate books and tag every SOW **EXPORT** or **LOCAL**.
5. **Competition is fragmented locally** (mostly small chatbot and automation boutiques, plus a few enterprise engineering firms such as Xtillion, Enalca and Involve) and **crowded for export**. The differentiators are production-grade delivery, governance and data readiness, GxP and banking vertical depth, and a **bilingual, onshore U.S., Eastern-time** team.
6. **Costs are people-driven.** Puerto Rico tech salaries are about **31–33% below** U.S. averages. A 5-person firm runs roughly **$750k/yr**. Startup cash needed is about **$50k–$110k**, plus runway.
7. **Permits are light:** Department of State ($150) → EIN → SURI merchant registration → municipal *patente* → CRIM → CFSE/Labor when hiring → DDEC decree, plus ASG RUL / PRITS RPT for government sales.
8. **Top risks:** sourcing and nexus errors, IRS and Senate scrutiny of Act 60 and founders' residency, winning export clients from Puerto Rico, talent competition, and grid and hurricane exposure.

## Time-sensitive items (as of Nov 22, 2026)

| Deadline | Item |
|---|---|
| **Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 p.m.** | File any founder's **Chapter 2** individual-investor application to lock in **0%** on dividends, interest and capital gains through 2035. Applications from Jan 1, 2027 get **4% through 2055**. The filing-fee payment timestamp on the DDEC portal is the proof. |
| Before first client | File the **Chapter 3** application at or right after formation, to avoid the **base-period** limitation. |
| Nov 15 – Dec 15 (yearly) | Pay the statutory **Christmas bonus** to eligible employees. |
| April 15 (yearly) | **LLC annual fee** ($150) to the Department of State. |
| Yearly | Act 60 **annual report** ($500 + $5 for Chapter 3). For tax year 2025 the deadline was **Nov 16, 2026** with DDEC; Chapter 2 reports move to Hacienda from tax year 2026. |

## Open questions to resolve with advisors

- Current DDEC **Chapter 3 application fee**, and any Act 52-2022 reporting changes.
- **IVU treatment** of export services, and the 4% vs 11.5% rate for local consulting.
- Single entity with segregated books, or separate export and local entities.
- Status of the **Young Entrepreneur** and **difficult-to-hire** incentives after recent amendments, and whether they combine with Chapter 3.
- Whether the **"Novel Pioneer Activity"** 1% rate is attainable for AI products.
- Enactment status of **SB 68** (Puerto Rico Government AI Act).
