# Market Overview

> **Research date:** November 22, 2026. Figures come from the cited sources. Survey statistics measure different populations, so read §2 before quoting any one number.

## TL;DR for agents

- Puerto Rico is a **two-speed AI market**. Large enterprises (banks, pharma, insurers, telecoms) are adopting generative AI quickly. Most small businesses are not yet using AI at all.
- The **U.S. Census Bureau (Oct 2026)** found only **15.7%** of Puerto Rico businesses use AI, against **24.6%** nationally. Puerto Rico sits near the bottom of U.S. states and territories, which signals both a gap and an opportunity.
- **IDC's CIO Playbook 2026** found **65%** generative-AI adoption among Puerto Rico companies (CIO-level, larger firms). **97%** plan to increase AI investment in the next 12 months, by about **+14%**. The main reported barrier is **data foundations**, not interest.
- The economy is anchored by **advanced manufacturing and life sciences**: pharma is about 30% of GDP and around $54B in annual life-sciences exports. These are regulated, data-rich, AI-ready industries.
- The best tax treatment (Act 60, 4% or 2%) applies only to **clients outside Puerto Rico**. The firm therefore operates in **two markets**: an **export market** (mainland U.S., LatAm, Europe) priced against mainland rates, and a **local market** taxed at regular rates. See [09-act-60-and-tax-benefits.md](09-act-60-and-tax-benefits.md).

## 2. AI adoption: reconciling the numbers

| Survey | Population | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **U.S. Census Bureau** Business Trends and Outlook Survey (reported Nov 2, 2026; October data) | Broad sample of employer businesses | **15.7%** of Puerto Rico businesses use AI vs **24.6%** nationally. Puerto Rico ranks near the bottom, alongside New Mexico (14.7%), Wyoming (14.4%), Idaho (13.5%) and West Virginia (9.8%). The top states are DC (34.1%), Utah (32.1%) and Colorado (31.9%). **28.6%** of surveyed businesses expect to use AI within six months, up from 21.1% a year earlier. |
| **Microsoft** SME survey (May 2025) | 100 Puerto Rico PyMEs (≤ 250 employees) | **32%** use some form of AI or GenAI; 52% are optimistic about AI's impact; reported benefits are process streamlining (49%), work quality (46%) and customer satisfaction (43%). A little over 50% intend to invest in AI, cybersecurity and digital tools. |
| **IDC Latin America**, *CIO Playbook 2026* (reported Apr 2026) | CIOs, mostly larger enterprises | **65%** GenAI adoption in Puerto Rico vs ~40% in Latin America; **97%** will increase AI investment in 12 months (+14% spend); **90%** expect positive ROI, about 3× the investment. Main inhibitor: **data structure and data-leak prevention**. Use cases cited: banking assistants and autonomous agents. |
| AI Assemblage (LinkedIn, 2026) | Not disclosed | "86% of organizations already using AI tools." The methodology is unknown, so do not rely on it. |

**How to read this.** Adoption is high and investment intent is strong among large enterprises, whose main gap is data readiness, governance and getting from pilot to production. Among the broad base of small businesses adoption is low (16–32%) but rising quickly. Both gaps are consulting opportunities, with different offers and price points (see [02-target-customers.md](02-target-customers.md)).

## 3. Public sector

- **Senate Bill 68 (PS 68), Puerto Rico Government AI Act** (proposed): would create an **AI Officer within PRITS** (Puerto Rico Innovation and Technology Service), require **biennial reviews** of agency AI systems, set standard procedures for buying and using automated decision systems, and create a **public registry of businesses using AI**. A related proposal would create an Institute for AI Development and Innovation. **Status: confirm** whether it has been enacted.
- Government buyers need governance, procurement-ready AI, document automation and bilingual citizen services. U.S. survey data shows public-sector AI use near 56%, with policies lagging at about 43%. Puerto Rico agencies probably lag that.
- Government work is local-market work. It is **not** eligible for the Act 60 4% rate, and procurement requires vendor registration (RUL and RPT; see [05-permits-and-licenses.md](05-permits-and-licenses.md)). Payment-timing risk is real.

## 4. Export market (the Act 60 opportunity)

The export market is the **entire U.S. mainland plus international clients**:
- Mainland **mid-market companies** that cannot get Big 4 or MBB attention and need AI strategy, agents, data platforms and LLM integration.
- **Life-sciences companies' global and mainland operations**, where the firm's Puerto Rico pharma experience is a credential. Work must not relate to the client's Puerto Rico operations to qualify.
- **Spanish-speaking and LatAm markets**, including U.S. Hispanic-serving businesses, where a bilingual Puerto Rico team is a real differentiator.
- **Pricing advantage.** Puerto Rico tech salaries run about **31–33% below** U.S. national averages (Glassdoor 2026). An onshore, U.S.-jurisdiction, same-time-zone (AST, UTC-4) team can bill close to mainland rates at a lower cost, and its profits are taxed at 2–4%.

## 5. Market sizing approach (for the finance agent)

No published Puerto Rico "AI consulting market size" figure was found. Build bottom-up:
1. **Local enterprise:** count Puerto Rico employers with more than 250 employees in banking, insurance, healthcare, pharma, telecoms, retail and utilities, then multiply by adoption intent (97%) × the share using outside consultants × average engagement value.
2. **Local SMEs:** about 96% of businesses × the 28.6% planning AI use within six months × a productized-package price.
3. **Export:** this is not constrained by Puerto Rico. Size by the number of target accounts the sales capacity can work (see [07-marketing.md](07-marketing.md)).
